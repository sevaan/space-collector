Space Collector

Privacy

Last updated 24 October 2026

The short version: your location is used to work out your sky. There are no accounts. Your collection stays on your phone and in your own phone backup (iCloud or Android). There are no ads, no tracking and no selling of data.

Your location

Space Collector needs to know roughly where you are to show what’s in your sky, and it works the sky out on your phone. On iPhone it asks for your precise location, so planes overhead are labelled in the right spot; you can turn Precise Location off when it asks, or later in Settings, and everything else works just the same. On Android it asks for approximate location only. One thing needs a rounded position (about 1 km to 10 km), which it shares with:

  • our server (at spacecollector.app, run on Google Cloud for Space Collector), which gets the weather forecast for your area from MET Norway. Aircraft near you, so the app can say “Just a plane”, come from the public flight-tracking network adsb.lol: in the app, your phone asks adsb.lol itself, with a rounded position; on the website, our server asks for you. To show where a flight is going, the app sends our server only that flight’s number (like ACA851); its usual route comes from Virtual Radar Server’s public route list. It doesn’t keep a record of where you were. Searching for a place by name in Settings happens on your phone, from a list of cities built into the app.

You can choose a place by hand instead in Settings, and the app works without your location at all (it shows an example sky).

Your collection

Your cards, sightings, patches and progress are stored on your phone. Each sighting records the time, what you saw and where you were standing, because that’s part of the card.

There’s no sign-up and no account. On iPhone your collection is also kept in your own iCloud (Apple’s iCloud key-value storage), so it comes back if you reinstall the app and matches on your iPad. On Android, the phone’s own backup to your Google account restores it on a new phone. We can’t see either copy.

Notifications

Reminders and alerts are scheduled on your phone. Nothing about them is sent to us.

Purchases

Space Collector Plus is sold through the App Store or Google Play. We don’t see your payment details. To know whether you have Plus, the app uses RevenueCat, a purchase service: it receives an anonymous ID for this install and your Plus purchase history from the store, and nothing else about you.

Usage and error reports

To learn whether the game works for people (does a first night end with a card? do people come back on other nights?), the app sends a few anonymous notes: when it’s opened, each night you catch something, how long a session lasted and how many cards it found, whether the phone’s motion sensors work, the tour finished or skipped, and errors that stop something working. They’re tied to a random ID made on your phone, not to you: no location, no name, no device identifier, nothing from your contacts or photos. They’re kept on our server (Google Cloud, in Space Collector’s own project) and used only to improve the game. You can turn them off any time in Settings → Help → Share anonymous usage.

What we don’t do

  • No ads and no advertising trackers.
  • No advertising or third-party analytics in the app; only the anonymous usage notes above, which you can turn off. The website (spacecollector.app) counts visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics, which uses no cookies and doesn’t track you across sites.
  • We never sell your data, and we share nothing beyond the rounded position described above.

Deleting your data

We don’t hold any of your data, so there’s nothing to delete on our side. Settings → Reset everything in the app deletes your collection from the phone and from your iCloud backup. Deleting the app removes what’s stored on the phone.

Children

Space Collector is made for everyone, including families stargazing together. There are no accounts, and we don’t collect personal information from anyone, children included.
