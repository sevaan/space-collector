Space Collector Plus

The sky stays free. Plus is for people who want to know what's coming and when, and want more depth once they're hooked. Nobody ever pays to catch, to complete a set, or to keep their collection safe.

The rule that decides every call below: charge for anticipation and depth (alerts, planning, radar, cosmetics); never charge for completion or safety (cards, odds, backup). In a collecting game, the sky decides rarity, not the wallet.

Free and Plus

Free everyone, forever

  • Unlimited catching and every card, set, shiny and gold card
  • All missions, streaks, achievements, ranks
  • Seasonal and meteor-shower event cards
  • ISS pass alerts, unlimited
  • "Come outside" alerts for the Moon and bright planets
  • 1 flyover alert a week for anything else, so you feel what Plus is like
  • Tonight planner: the next 24 hours, best passes flagged, Show me
  • Cloud forecast, sky slider, binocular mode, plane ID
  • Collection backup (export now; free sync when accounts arrive)
  • 1 free streak freeze a month (cloudy weeks aren't your fault)

Plus $24.99 / year

  • Unlimited flyover alerts for any satellite, fleet or rare object; a wanted list
  • New-train and fresh-launch alerts ("Starlink train tonight, 9:42 PM, NW")
  • Shiny Radar: alerts for shadow entries, Moon crossings and Epic/Legendary passes
  • 7-night planner with the best night picked, plus photographer-grade Sun/Moon transit predictions
  • Every alert is clear-sky aware: it only fires if your forecast is clear
  • Foil and holo finishes, premium card backs, holographic share cards, album themes
  • Stats, logbook and export
  • Sync across devices; Family Sharing on (each kid keeps their own collection)

Never behind the paywall

  • Catching anything, or any card that counts toward completing a set. Event cards are free; Plus gets a foil version of them.
  • Shiny odds, XP boosts or anything that makes catches easier ("every catch counts").
  • Backup of your collection. "Lost 200 cards, they want $4" is a one-star review in one sentence.
  • Upsell prompts in a child's view. Locked shinies shown to an 8-year-old read as a loot box.
  • Ads anywhere near the catch screen.

How people get a taste

You wanted free users to experience the paid features and like them before they're asked to pay. The room agreed, with three adjustments:

  • The ISS stays free. It was the most-loved moment in the room ("I'll call my wife out to the porch"), and it's what spreads by word of mouth. The Moon and bright planets join it, for the same reason.
  • One free flyover alert a week, and the app suggests a great one: a fresh Starlink train or a shiny chance. That way the free taste is the best version of Plus, not a dull pass. The weekly counter only appears once someone has used an alert.
  • The trial runs on clear nights, not days. A 7-day trial in a cloudy week converts the forecast, not the product. The first time someone taps a Plus feature, they get Plus for their next 3 clear nights, unlocked by the app itself. After that, the store trial is 14 days, annual plan only.

Revised build order

This changes what I suggested earlier (accounts, then native apps, then paid). All three panels pushed the same way: test whether anyone pays before building the plumbing. Otherwise, if the first cloudy month shows 1% conversion, you can't tell whether price, product or weather is to blame.

  1. Measure and fake-door test on the web, now. Add privacy-friendly analytics. Put a "Plus" sheet behind Shiny Radar and the 7-night planner and record which locked feature people tap. Sell a real "Founding Plus" for $14.99/yr through a Stripe link, with alerts delivered to founders first. Add free collection export/import so nobody can lose their cards.
  2. Native iPhone app with free alerts. Capacitor around the existing app. Alerts are predicted on the phone and scheduled as local notifications, so there's no server bill and no stale location. Each alert is gated on sunlit, dark, bright enough and clear forecast; at most one per night; permission is asked after your first catch, not at launch.
  3. Plus in the stores. RevenueCat, annual first, the clear-night taste, the 14-day annual trial, Family Sharing on. Founding web buyers get a code to unlock it in the app.
  4. Android. Cheap once iOS works.
  5. Accounts and sync, when paying users ask for multiple devices. Shiny Radar and event pushes then move to a small server that works per ~50 km area, not per person.

Risks to watch

  • Seasons. People subscribe in a clear spell and cancel in November. Lead with annual; the clear-night taste protects the trial.
  • Novelty. After the 20th Starlink, satellites blur together. Rare objects, events and the radar are the retention engine. That's another reason not to lock the long tail.
  • Alert fatigue. A pass behind cloud, or three pings in a night, and people switch alerts off for good. Clear-sky gating and one-a-night are part of the product, not polish.
  • Small funnel. Casual stargazers are a niche, and summer nights are short up here. Plan on 1–3% of monthly players paying.
