Inside or out

The tour doesn’t know where the player is. Most people install it indoors and by day, yet two of our best first-night ideas only work for someone outside in the dark. This board adds one quick question to the tour and shows what changes for each answer. It also covers a separate “match your sky” moment that waits until the player really is outside at night.

How to read it: each mock-up sits on a real capture of today’s app (Toronto, Oct 25, a fresh player), with the proposed part drawn on top. Grey Today captions show the app as it is now. Orange captions are the proposal, and gold marks my pick. Times and passes are examples. Sister board: The first night.

The question · one tap that tells the tour where you are

01“Where are you right now?”

A card with two big choices, Inside and Outside, each with a small line drawing, plus a quiet Not sure link underneath. No permission is asked and nothing can go wrong, so it’s one tap. The answer changes the copy on the motion card, the first target and the last page of the tour. The clock and location already tell us whether it’s dark.

Where it goes: A puts it first. B puts it after location, which I prefer. Once the place is known, the card can say real things (“2:14 PM in Toronto · dark at 6:43 PM”). The location ask also still comes while the player is paying full attention, and the woken sky is already behind the card.

4 · How it ends

Both cards, then what’s still to come tonight. The notification ask lives on this page’s button. The one-time lesson panel plays on the ISS reveal before this.

Missed it? If the pass is lost (behind a tree, under cloud, or they wandered off), the pill reads “Missed it · next pass Tue 6:12 AM” for a minute and the tour ends on their Moon. It isn’t a failure, just a date. No big pass within 15 min: today’s card (“Catch the Moon · Low in the east”) runs unchanged.

Later, outside · the sky slider, matched to the real sky

05“Match your sky”, once, when they’re really out

Replaces the old “compare it with Orion” idea, and isn’t part of the tour. It shows once, the first time the app is open after dark with motion live and the phone held above about 20° for about 10 seconds (so they’re outside, looking up) with nothing in the circle. A small card sits just above the sky slider with an arrow pointing down at it, and the knob pulses. They slide until the screen looks like the sky, and the readout says what that means.

Why this beats the Orion idea
  • Works facing any direction. Whatever is on screen is compared with whatever is above you, so no constellation has to be up, found or known.
  • Only asks when the answer is real. After dark, motion live and the phone held high for ten seconds means they’re outside looking up. It never shows indoors or by day, where the comparison would be nonsense.
  • Uses what’s already built. The slider fades stars live while dragging and reruns Tonight on release. The new parts are the trigger, the card and a sky {s:'match', sb} note.

Today: the slider sits there unlabelled except SEE LESS / SEE MORE, and new players start halfway (Suburbs). Nothing ever asks them to set it.

Keeping it short · still three steps

07Three steps, not four

Adding a question shouldn’t make the tour longer. Proposed: 1 Find your sky · 2 Where are you? · 3 Point and look. Notifications move out of the tour to the button on the last page (“Tell me at 7:42 PM” / “Tell me when to look up”). There the ask comes with a reason the player has just seen, which is the same move the appointment page already makes for the ISS. The fork is a single tap with no system prompt, so it costs less than the step it replaces.

SmallReorder · the ask moves

The other way: keep 4 steps (location, fork, motion, notifications). It’s simpler to build, and the extra step is a quick tap. But each system prompt in a row loses people, and an early notification ask has no reason behind it yet. Our own /stats can settle it: compare tour finish rate and day-one notification opt-in before and after.

What I’d do

In this order, smallest first.

  1. 01 B · The question, after location, no defaultOne card and three notes. Everything else hangs off the answer, and the data tells us if the rest is worth it.
  2. 02 + 04 · The words for inside and by dayCopy on cards we already have, and the appointment page we built today. This covers most first runs.
  3. 07 · Notifications out of the tour, onto the last pageWatch the opt-in rate; put it back if it drops.
  4. 05 · Match your skyA trigger and a card on the existing slider, with no tour changes.
  5. 03 · Rare-first at nightThe rarest case (outside, dark, and a big pass inside 15 minutes) and the riskiest logic: wait, switch targets, and recover from a miss. Build it last and judge it by first: 'iss'.

Open questions

Things I’d want Sevaan’s call on before building.

Should “Not sure” exist?
I’d keep it, small. Some people are on a balcony or by a window, and a forced choice makes them guess. It behaves like Inside, whose copy is safe anywhere. If under 5% use it, drop it.
They say Outside, but it’s cloudy
The forecast already knows. Outside under cloud should behave like Inside, with copy that admits it: “Cloudy right now. The Moon’s behind it, but it still counts.” No rare-first, because a countdown to something they can’t see is a let-down.
Inside at night, then they walk out
Nothing to redo in the tour. The Match card (05) is the one that catches them, because its trigger is looking up after dark, not the tour answer. Rare-first is a first-night-only treat, so they miss it. Fine.
Do we trust the answer for good?
No. It’s only for the tour and the first night. After that, behaviour (looking up, after dark, for a while) is the signal, as in 05. We never ask again.
Rare-first: is 15 minutes right?
Long enough that it happens sometimes, short enough that the Moon fills the wait. Notes would tell us how often it fires at all. In late October there are no evening ISS passes over Toronto, so it would mostly be other Rares.
Location refused, at night, “Outside”
The time-zone city’s sky is close but not theirs, so a countdown to a pass could be minutes off. I’d turn rare-first off without a real location.
Match card and the slider’s day state
By day the slider is hidden, so the card can’t show by day anyway. If the Moon is bright, the slider readout already counts it in; the card should say “The Moon’s washing out the faint ones” when it’s over 70% lit.
Notification ask on the last page
If they close the page with the ✕, they’re never asked. Is the Settings row plus the first reminder bell enough of a second chance? I think yes.
