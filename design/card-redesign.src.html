A new card front, three ways

Every card type, drawn with the game's real art, in three styles. The art window has a fixed shape in every style, so pictures are never squished, only cropped.

Thinking first

What a card should carry

Rules for every card

  • The picture is the hero: at least 40% of the card, one fixed shape per style, cropped (never stretched). Rocket stage and fleet cards stop shrinking their art.
  • Name, then what it is in plain words: "Space telescope · NASA and ESA · 1990".
  • Four numbers chosen per type (height, speed, laps a day, launched for satellites; distance, size, temperature for stars).
  • One fact on the front. The rest go on the back as Field notes, unlocked by nights seen.
  • Footer: collector number, collected date or "not yet", and level.
  • Rarity as both a colour and a shape (● ▲ ◆ ✦ ★), as now.

Extra info worth adding

  • What it does for every satellite we know (UCS database, series and name families cover most; the rest say "purpose not public").
  • Who built it and the country, with the launch site.
  • How far north and south it flies ("Reaches 28.5° N" tells you why Hubble is always low from Canada).
  • Size and mass where known ("as long as a bus").
  • Status: working, dead, or falling (last chance).
  • Stars: colour as temperature, distance in light years, size next to the Sun.
  • Planets: distance tonight, which changes; moons; length of day and year.

Story cards: a new type

  • For moments and history, not objects. Collected by finding a secret.
  • Make these story cards: the Wow! signal, Voyager 1's Pale Blue Dot, Voyager 2's Grand Tour, the Pioneer plaque, New Horizons' heart of Pluto, the Arecibo message, Sputnik's beep, Laika, Apollo 11, the lost tool bag, Ed White's glove, Halley's way home, Two homes (ISS + Tiangong).
  • Keep as patches only (they're jokes, not stories): the UFO, Santa, dinosaur bones, Dizzy, the 3 AM owl.
  • Galaxies are already cards (Andromeda, Triangulum and both Magellanic Clouds); the new design gives them full-size art.

Style A

Mission card

The app's navy and cream with the card back's double frame. Big 4:3 picture on top, the rarity on the picture, numbers in a strip. Closest to today, much more picture.

Style B

Trading card

A thick metallic frame in the rarity colour, so a Legendary looks Legendary at a glance. Numbers as little meters, the fact as flavour text. The most "collectable card game".

Style C

Poster

The picture fills the whole card, like the card back, and the words sit on a dark band at the bottom. The most striking, but our pictures are landscape, so here they're cropped hard. This style would need every picture redrawn tall, which is the big cost.

My pick: A for the everyday cards, with B's metallic rarity frame on Epic and Legendary only. A reads best at small sizes in the collection grid and keeps the app's look; the frame makes rare finds feel rare. C is a lovely special treatment for story cards. Numbers here are real where I know them; Cosmos 2558's fall date is an example.
