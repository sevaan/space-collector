All around you, redesigned

The page the radar opens. Today: a big heading number, a strip of the horizon, four boxes and two lists. Four fresh takes on the same job — what's worth looking at right now, and which way — each shown at night with the Moon, Saturn and two new satellites up, the phone facing southeast.

A · A big window

The strip grows into the page: a tall window onto your horizon, the way you face in the middle, and everything worth a look shown as its own card picture on the sky, not a dot. The big number goes; "Facing SE" floats at the top. Under it, the same cards as a row you can swipe, then three small facts.

B · The whole sky as a dome

The sky from above: the rim is the horizon, the middle is straight overhead, and it turns with you (where you face is always at the top, under an orange wedge). Things worth a look sit at their real spot as card pictures. It's the only view that also shows what's right above you. A list under it says which way to turn.

D · A compass ring

A real compass: the ring turns with you, your heading in the middle. Each thing worth a look is a bar pointing out from the ring in its direction — the longer the bar, the higher it is in the sky — with its name at the end. Reads at a glance outside: which way, and how high.

My pick: A. It keeps what's working (the horizon strip, turning with you, tilting) but makes it the page, and uses the card pictures so you can tell at a glance what each thing is and whether it's new. C is the most helpful for a beginner; B shows overhead but is harder to match to the real sky; D is the quickest read but the least pretty.
