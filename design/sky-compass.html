Tap the compass: a big one

Tapping the little dial top left opens a full page, made for one job: where is everything above me right now, and which way do I turn? Everything turns with the phone (the way you face is always at the top), and tapping anything on it closes the page and aims you there, like Show me. Four takes.

A · Sky dome

The whole sky as a circle: the rim is the horizon, the centre is straight overhead, rings at 30° and 60°. Everything up now is drawn as its sky icon at its real spot, new ones ringed orange. Your view is a soft wedge. Under it: what’s up, nearest your view first.

B · Compass rose

A proper compass first: your heading huge in the middle (“247° WSW”), a fine rose turning with you. Things in the sky sit as pins round the rim at their direction, with how high to look on each pin. The Sun and Moon’s rise and set points are marked on the rim too.

C · Radar sweep

The little heat radar, grown up: a beam sweeps round and each thing pings and names itself as it passes. Tonight’s next passes are drawn as tracks across it with times, so you see both now and the next half hour. The most alive; the busiest to read.

D · All around you

Not a circle: a 360° strip of your horizon, the way you face in the middle, with height marks up the side. Things sit at their real direction and height, so it reads like the view itself, unrolled. Drag it round; tap anything to aim. Easiest to match to the real sky; least like a compass.

My pick: A, with B’s heading. The dome is the one view that answers “what’s above me” in one look, including straight overhead, which a rose (B) or a strip (D) can’t show. Add B’s big heading number and the compass-quality chip at the top, and it doubles as a real compass for checking the phone is pointing true. C is fun but hard to read on a dark night; D is the easiest to match to the sky but loses what’s behind you.
