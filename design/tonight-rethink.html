Tonight: what it needs to be

A room of three UX designers and five players looked at today's page (welcome card, strip chart, Up now hero, chips, an 11-stop run of near-identical Kosmos rows, missions, all passes). Their notes, the takeaways, then three redesigns.

Mayaparent, checks at 5 pm

"I just want to know: is tonight worth going out, and when exactly. I had to read a chart and a sentence and still wasn't sure. Give me a yes or no and a time."

Devcompletionist

"Eleven rows of 'Kosmos something · Uncommon · +15'. They're all the same to me. Tell me which three are worth it and why: the rare one, the one that finishes a set, the one that's leaving forever."

Anaoutside, phone in hand

"When I'm actually out there I don't scroll. I need the next thing, huge: what, where to point, how long. Then the one after."

Jordan13, plays games

"Missions are at the very bottom and look like a to-do list. In games the daily stuff is the first thing you see, with the rewards."

Samlapsed after a week

"Every night looked the same, so I stopped checking. I'd come back for something that only happens tonight, or a streak I don't want to break."

UX lead

"Two heroes fight at the top (Night 2 welcome vs Up now). The page serves two jobs, planning by day and hunting by night, with one layout. Split them by time."

Visual designer

"Names like Kosmos 1741 mean nothing. The card art is our best asset and it's hidden. Show cards, not text rows; say why each matters in plain words."

Product designer

"The return hook is weak: nothing changes day to day except numbers. One thing that's unique to tonight plus a visible week streak gives people a reason to open it."

What the page must do
  1. Answer "should I go out, and when?" in one glance: a verdict with a time window.
  2. Pick for me. At most three or four picks, each with a reason (rare, finishes a set, last chance, headliner). Everything else lives in "All passes".
  3. Two modes. By day it's a plan; outside at night it's a live "next up" with a big countdown and pointer.
  4. Lead with rewards. Missions and the night's unique thing at the top, not the bottom.
  5. Show cards, not rows. Real (obscured) card art, plain-language reasons.
  6. A reason to come back: tonight-only items, a visible week streak, and a teaser for tomorrow.

A · Verdict and three picks Recommended

One verdict card answers the question (green "Go out", amber "Short window", grey "Cloudy"), with the best window and one button. Then three picks with reasons (headliner, rarest, finishes a set), each a real card. Missions sit as pills on the picks that complete them. A small week strip at the foot shows your streak and tomorrow.
