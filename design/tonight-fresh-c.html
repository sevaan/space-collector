Tonight, in shifts

A fresh take on the Tonight page, drawn from one real night over Peterborough: Thursday 29 October 2026. Overcast all evening, clear only from midnight to 2 AM (when almost no satellite is lit), patchy cloud for the Space Station at 6:07 AM. Every pass, time, direction and cloud figure below is real. Planet positions come from the app's own celestial.js.

What tonight-combined gets wrong (from the field)
  • It's drawn for a perfect night that didn't happen. "Clear, and busy" is the hero, and cloud gets one footnote ("picks greyed, if it clears"). In Ontario in October, cloud is the normal case. Run tonight's real sky through it and three of its four states just say "no".
  • Earth's shadow reads as dead air. "Nothing for 15+ min" shrinks the page to one line. But 9:30 PM to 4 AM is the longest, darkest stretch of the night, and tonight it's the only clear part. That's exactly when the page goes quiet.
  • The surest catches aren't on it. The Moon and planets are up every night, whatever the satellite count. They only show up as a past "✓ Saturn". A page about the sky shouldn't be satellites-only.
  • The hand can't reach the action. "Aim at it" sits halfway down a 15-part column. With one cold hand and gloves, the thumb lives in the bottom third, and that's where the week strip is. Here the one action always sits there.
  • Direction comes as text or a puzzle. "southwest", then "Turn left, then up" with an arrow that only makes sense once you've read three lines. And a 6:08 AM headliner sits above everything else at 10:40 PM, so "Good night" is said while the best thing is still 7 hours away.
The concept

The real sky runs the night in three shifts, and the page follows them. At the Dusk shift the satellites are lit by a Sun you can't see, and they slip into Earth's shadow one by one, which makes it the shiny rush. During the Shadow shift (Planet hour), Earth's shadow covers the satellite sky and the Moon, planets and stars take over, with their own quest. The Dawn shift is when the satellites come back before sunrise. The page always opens on the shift you're in, or the next good one. It does one job in big type, with the button under your thumb. Cloud is drawn over the shifts as a real layer, so it never wipes the plan. It moves the play to the clear part, and anything spotted through a gap still counts. Tonight that turns an "overcast, sorry" into "your window is midnight, it's Planet hour, here's the quest".

Red night mode · Planet hour, as the eye sees it
  • Nothing relies on colour alone. Rarity keeps its shape (● ▲ ◆ ✦ ★). Cloud is hatching, not a grey tone. Earth's shadow has a dashed edge. The "now" line is a solid bar. All of it survives the red multiply, and it also helps colour-blind players in daylight.
  • One bright thing per screen. The dock button is the only solid fill, so a dark-adapted eye lands on it, and on nothing else. Panels stay near black, with text at full red, never dim brown.
  • Offer it, don't force it. When Planet hour starts and you're aiming at a real dark sky, the shift card shows one chip: "Going dark? Turn on red". No auto-switch (the after-dark auto mode was removed on 2026-10-21).
  • Gloves and cold. 50px targets in the bottom 170px, no swipes needed for the main job, and the countdown and direction readable at arm's length (52px numbers).
