Real sky, real situations

Five changes for the player who’s standing in an actual backyard, a bright city, or behind a window, and for the moments at night when the screen should say a little more. Each idea shows what the app does today and what I’d change.

How to read the mock-ups: the phones are the real Explore screen at 90% size, with sky backgrounds captured from the app tonight (Toronto area, 9:15 PM) and real card art. Today phones are the current app; Proposed or A / B are the change. Effort: small is an afternoon, medium a day or two, large more.

Works for the player’s real situation · bright skies, wrong compasses, stuck indoors

01Which looks like your sky?

The sky slider asks people to judge “how dark is your sky” with no reference, so most leave it at the default. Instead, on the first night, show three small pictures of Orion as it looks from a city, the suburbs and the countryside (the faint stars drop out as the sky gets brighter). Tap the one that matches what you see and the slider jumps there. This is the Globe at Night method (globeatnight.org): since 2006 people have measured light pollution by matching Orion to star charts of different limiting magnitudes. We use three charts, not seven. When Orion isn’t up in the evening (about April to October), the Big Dipper takes its place.

MediumTour step · Settings
ProposedAs a tour card

A fourth tour card after Location, before the first catch. “Not sure” leaves the slider halfway, as now. The pictures use the real positions and brightness of Orion’s stars; stars fainter than 2.5 (city), 4 (suburbs) and 6 (countryside) are left out.

ProposedThe sky wakes up set

Today: the slider sits halfway for everyone, so a city player sees stars on screen that aren’t in their sky, and a countryside player misses faint satellites. Here the slider glides to the picked spot as the sky wakes, then rests.

ProposedSettings → Your sky

One new row under the slider. Its value says what you last picked, so the slider and the picture never disagree in words.

ProposedThe sheet, Big Dipper season

From Settings, the same choice as a sheet. In late October evenings Orion isn’t up until after 11 PM, so it opens on the Big Dipper, low in the north; the chips let you switch.

02Moon check

The compass on many phones is off by 5–20°, and the player has no way to know. The Moon is the one thing everyone can find in the real sky. So the first time someone catches the Moon, after the reveal, the ticket under the circle asks one plain question: is the real Moon in the circle? Yes ends it. “It’s off” opens Line up the sky already pointed at the Moon, which is the reference it would pick anyway.

SmallUses Line up the sky
TodayAfter the Moon’s reveal

Today: you’re back on the sky with View card. If the drawn Moon is 10° to the side of the real one, nothing says so, and every satellite after it is off by the same amount.

ProposedThe one-time question

Shown once, in place of the View card ticket, about a second after the reveal closes. It only shows if the Moon is 10°+ up. Ignoring it (turning away) counts as no answer and it asks again next Moon.

Proposed“It’s off” → Line up the sky

Opens the existing Line up flow on step A with the Moon as the reference. The drawn Moon stays where the app thinks it is, with a dashed ring, so the gap is obvious.

ProposedLined up

The usual “Sky lined up” notice and the View card ticket back. Settings → Aiming → Last lined up reads “Against the Moon · just now”.

03Window mode

Lots of people will try this from inside: a balcony door, a bedroom window, a car. Today the app happily points at a satellite behind the wall. Window mode asks which way your window faces, then the sky only shows that slice (the rest is hatched as wall), and Tonight lists only the passes that cross it: “3 passes through your window tonight”. It also explains why some nights look empty from inside.

LargeSettings · Explore · Tonight
APicker: four directions

The simplest. Four big buttons and “Use my phone”: hold the phone flat against the glass and tap, and it reads the heading. A window is treated as 90° wide.

BPicker: eight directions

A dial with eight slices and a width choice (narrow window 60°, wide 100°). More exact, a little more to think about. My pick: A with the phone button, since the phone knows better than the player.

ProposedThe sky through the window

Past the window’s edge the sky is hatched and dimmed, and nothing there can be targeted. The radar shows the window as a lit wedge. A chip under the header says the mode is on; ✕ turns it off.

ProposedTonight, filtered

Rows show the minutes each pass is inside the window, not its whole pass. The rest are one tap away, never hidden for good.

Clarity at night · when the screen should say a little more

04Empty-sky nudge

A new player often points at a patch of sky with nothing in it, waits, and decides the app doesn’t work. After about 20 seconds at night with nothing in the circle, a gentle line under the circle says so and points to where the action is: “Nothing lit here · 3 up to the east ›”. Tapping it steers you there with the normal arrow. It counts only new cards that are lit right now, and it goes away the moment you move.

SmallExplore
Today20 seconds of nothing

Today: an empty circle and silence. The radar does show the glows, but new players don’t read it yet.

AA ticket, like the others

Uses the same dark ticket as every other state under the circle, in the grey “guidance” colour, so it reads as part of the system. The whole ticket is the button.

BOne quiet line + edge arrow

Lighter: one line of text under the circle and the pulsing orange edge arrow on the side to turn. Less to read; easier to miss. My pick: A for the first three nights, then B.

ProposedAfter the tap

The usual steering ticket and edge arrow, aimed at the rarest of the three. When it reaches the circle, the normal Collect ticket takes over.

05Teach the fade, live

Catch the fade is the best skill in the game, and nobody finds it on their own. The gold ring that runs down (“FADES IN 8S”) means nothing the first time. So the first time the countdown ring appears, one line explains it right there: “It’s about to slip into Earth’s shadow. Tap Collect just as it vanishes for a shiny.” Then the shiny notice says what you did, so it sticks.

SmallExplore · notice
AA callout above the circle

Points at the ring itself, so the ring and the words connect. Sits between the header and the circle, where nothing else lives at night.

BInside the ticket

The ticket’s meta line turns gold and says it. Nothing new on screen, but it’s further from the ring, and the taller ticket pushes the sky slider aside (as tall tickets already do). My pick: A.

ProposedThe last three seconds

The ring brightening to NOW! and the object fading are today’s behaviour. With the line above, the player now knows this is the moment. The line stays until the tap or the fade.

ProposedThe shiny notice

After the reveal, the notice names the move: “You caught it as it slipped into Earth’s shadow.” Today the reveal says SHINY but nothing says what earned it. If the tap was late, a one-time line instead: “Just missed it. Next time tap as it vanishes.”

Order I’d build them

Smallest and most useful first.

  1. 05 · Teach the fadeAn afternoon. Turns the best skill in the game from a secret into something every player tries on their first fade.
  2. 02 · Moon checkSmall, because Line up the sky already exists. It finds wrong compasses on the night they matter, instead of never.
  3. 04 · Empty-sky nudgeSmall. Rescues the “it doesn’t work” first night.
  4. 01 · Which looks like your sky?Medium. The pictures are the work; the slider mapping is already there.
  5. 03 · Window modeLarge: it touches targeting, the radar and the Tonight planner. Worth doing once the others are in.

Notes. Slider mapping for 01: City → 17.5 (Bright city), Suburbs → 19.45 (today’s default), Countryside → 21.3; Globe at Night publishes the matching limiting magnitudes.
