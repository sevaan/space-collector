Tonight, as a sky forecast

A fresh take from the weather-app side of the room (Dark Sky, Apple Weather, Flighty). Built on tonight's real data over Peterborough: overcast until 11 PM (Tiangong at 7:20 PM behind it), clear midnight to 2 AM while almost nothing is sunlit, and the Space Station at 6:07 AM under about 40% cloud.

What tonight-combined gets wrong

  • It's a feed, not a forecast. Verdict, picks, list. There's no time axis, so "when" is one window string and you never see the shape of the night.
  • Weather comes last. Cloud is a footnote ("same layout, greyed"). It has no way to say tonight's truth: clear, but nothing's lit. Clear sky and catchable sky aren't the same thing.
  • No odds. A 40%-cloud ISS and a sure thing look identical. Weather apps earned trust with "60% chance"; we say "Go out tonight".
  • The page rearranges itself. Plan, then live takeover, then wrap-up: three layouts in one evening. Nothing sits in the same spot two visits running, so it can't be glanced at.
  • Card thumbnails crowd out the answer. Three 54px cards with stacked pills fill most of the screen before you learn when to go out.

The concept: the Night Ribbon

The page is one fixed instrument, like the precipitation graph in Dark Sky. A ribbon from dusk to dawn shows cloud hanging from the top as a grey ceiling, satellites still in sunlight rising from the bottom as bars, and Earth's shadow as a dark band across the middle of the night. Where a bar reaches clear sky it turns orange: that's what you can actually catch. One sentence above it says what the ribbon shows. Below it sit at most three windows with a clear-sky chance, what's in each and a verdict (Go / Maybe / Skip), and one alarm. The layout never changes: morning, evening, midnight or dawn, only the NOW line moves and the sentence is rewritten. Live "next up" is a slim card under the ribbon, never a takeover.
