Explore · looking down

Three ground compasses

The same sky in all three: you're facing south-east, with Saturn, Vega, Capella, Uranus, three new satellites and a Globalstar up. The goal: glance down, see what's worth turning to and which way, then raise the phone. The sketches turn slowly, as if you were turning.

A · Clock face

Each thing is a spoke on the ring. Its length is how high it is, so taller spokes mean look higher.

  • Facing wedge at the top, so "ahead" is always up the screen.
  • Things within 15° of each other share one spoke ("3 new satellites"), so labels never stack.
  • New cards get an orange spoke; stars are thin cream ones.

B · Heading tape

A strip across the screen like a plane's heading tape. Things sit on it by direction, with their height stacked above.

  • Reads left to right, and neighbours merge into one marker, so labels never tangle.
  • The centre mark is where you face; "turn left 40°" is just how far a thing is from it.
  • Off the edges, arrows show how many things are behind you.

C · Sky map at your feet

A small all-sky map on the ground: the edge is the horizon, the centre is straight up, things sit where they really are.

  • Same picture as a printed star chart: height is distance from the edge.
  • Icons, not text, for most things; only the best 3 are named and a tight bunch gets one label.
  • The wedge shows where you're facing, and turns with you.

A + C · Sky map in a compass ring

C's map inside A's ring. Each thing sits where it really is (edge = horizon, centre = straight up), and a short spoke carries its name out past the ring, so labels never sit on top of the map.

  • Inside: the true sky, so you can see height at a glance: near the rim is low, near the middle is high.
  • Outside: the names, on spokes at their compass direction, like A. A thin line joins each dot to its spoke.
  • Bunches share one spoke with a count ("3 new satellites"); stars stay as small dots, and only planets, the Moon and new cards get a name.
  • The wedge shows where you're facing and the whole dial turns with you.
My pick: B, the heading tape. It's the easiest to read at a glance and can't get tangled however many things are up, which is the problem in your screenshot. A is the prettiest; C matches the old map, but it's busier.
