Below the horizon · what the circle says

Today the circle shows: collected or not, the name, what it is, where it is over the Earth, its height, its speed, how far away it is, and when it's next over you. Each option keeps all of that except the distance, which only B keeps a place for (as a fifth cell if wanted). They differ in how much reads at a glance. Example: COSMOS 2322.

B · Data card

Everything sits in one glass card under the circle, in the same style as the toasts. Small labels and big values, laid out two by two, so each fact is one number. The sky around the circle is left clear.

C · Around the ring

The facts sit around the circle like an instrument readout: height on the left, speed on the right, place below. The name and a short "1995 · Russian satellite · new" sit above. Light, but it spreads text across more of the sky.

COSMOS 2322NEW
A 1995 Russian satellite, 850 km above the North Pacific and racing at 26,800 km/h. It's over you at 4:14 PM.

D · One sentence

The facts are written as one short sentence in normal type, the way a guide would say it. It reads as a story rather than a spec sheet. Distance is left out. Slowest to scan, but it feels the calmest.
