Sky slider: city to dark site, then binoculars

Four ways to make the one slider run from a lit-up city to a pristine dark site and then keep going into binoculars. Drag each one: the little sky above it fills in exactly as the game's does (the app's own formula). Binoculars add about 3 magnitudes for satellites and for Uranus and Neptune; the stars themselves stay at your sky's limit.

One thing to decide: binoculars aren't really darker sky, they're a tool. In A, B and C, reaching binoculars means a dark site plus binoculars, so "city with binoculars" goes away. D keeps them separate and still reads as one scale.

AOne long track

The track runs City → Dark site, then past a notch into a dashed orange zone. Slide the thumb into it and it turns into binoculars.

  • Simplest to explain: further right, more to see.
  • The notch makes the switch into binoculars feel deliberate.

BPull past the end

A normal slider that stops at Dark site. Keep dragging past the end and the track stretches, "keep pulling for binoculars" appears, and letting go there locks it on.

Keep pulling for binoculars →

  • A small secret that rewards curiosity.
  • Easy to miss; it would need a first-time hint.

CFive stops

Five tappable stops, each with a picture: city lights, a house, trees, mountains, then binoculars. Drag along them or tap one.

  • Clearest at a glance, and easy to tap in the dark.
  • Loses the fine-grained in-between settings.

DSlider plus lens

The darkness slider with a binocular lens at its end. Tap the lens and a striped boost extends past the fill, wherever the slider sits.

CitySuburbsDark site

  • Keeps city + binoculars, which is real.
  • Two controls, but they read as one scale.

My pick: A, with D's idea as a fallback if city + binoculars matters. A is one gesture with one meaning ("further right, more to see"), and the notch stops anyone sliding into binoculars by accident.
