The catch, the feel

Six ideas to make the core loop feel better: aim, catch, keep going. Three change how a catch plays out (quick Commons, chains, the peak bonus); three are about what you notice around it (what's worth aiming at, the night's sound, and how many notices a first night throws at you). Each has today beside the proposal.

How to read the mock-ups: the ◉ tags light up orange when the phone would buzz, ♪ when a sound would play. The ▶ buttons play the game's real sounds. Cards, art, icons and the target circle are the app's own (sizes and colours copied from css/reveal.css, css/app.css and js/sky.js); the two “Today” screenshots are captures of the real app. Effort: small an afternoon, medium a day or two, large more.

A core loop that feels good · catch more, stop less

01Quick Commons

A Common you've never had snaps into a little stack under the circle instead of opening the full sealed-card reveal: a mini card pops out of the circle, drops onto the stack with a click and a buzz, and the count says “+3 tonight”. You never leave the sky. Uncommon and up keep the whole reveal, so the big moments stay big. The first card you ever catch, and the first of each kind (first star, first stage, the Moon), always get the full reveal.

Tapping the stack fans out tonight's quick catches; tap one and it grows into the full card (flip, tilt, share, as in the viewer). The stack sits under the circle all night and empties at dawn. Long-press it to send it away early.

Which reveal you get

  • Your very first card: full reveal, always.
  • First of a kind (first star, stage, planet, Moon, fleet): full reveal.
  • Common: quick stack.
  • Uncommon · Rare · Epic · Legendary: full reveal, unchanged.
  • Shiny, rank-up or patch on a Common: full reveal.
  • Settings → “Show every card” for people who love the flip.

02See what’s worth aiming at

In the sky, everything looks the same today: a satellite you've had for weeks is as bright as one you've never caught. Give the ones new to you a soft halo in their rarity colour that breathes slowly, and draw the ones you own a little dimmer. You can scan a patch of sky and know where to point before anything is in the circle.

Rules

  • New card: full-strength icon plus a breathing halo (18 px, tier colour, 2.8 s). Epic/Legendary halos a touch bigger.
  • New stamp on a fleet or Moon phase you own: a smaller, plain cream halo.

Cheap to draw: one cached glow sprite per tier, stamped like the radar's heat glows. Same rule could light the dots on All around you.

03Chains

Catching two or three new cards within one pass window builds a chain. A small counter appears beside the circle with a ring that runs down; catch another before it empties and the chain grows: Chain ×2 · +10 XP, ×3 · +20. On the reveal the chain shows as a chip next to the card's XP. Busy windows (a Starlink train, the 9 PM rush) become a little game of keeping it alive.

The chain chip uses the reveal's existing news row (.rv-chip), so it lands with the same tick as missions. With Quick Commons, a chain on Commons shows on the stack instead (“×2” on the counter).

Rules

  • Only new cards (and new stamps) count. Repeat sightings don't break it or add to it.
  • Window: 90 s after each catch, the ring's length. Long enough to turn to the next pass, short enough to feel live.
  • Reward: ×2 +10, ×3 +20, ×4 +30, then +30 each. Cap at ×6.
  • The counter hides under pages and the reveal; its clock keeps going.
  • Best chain goes in Observer stats; a patch at ×5 (“Rush Hour”).
  • Not during the tour, so the first catch stays calm.

Today: each catch is scored alone (10 / 15 / 25 / 40 / 60 XP by tier). Nothing rewards a good run.

04Peak bonus

Every pass has a highest point. Mark it on the pass's path with a small tick, and catching within 30 s either side of it gives “Caught at its peak · +10 XP”. It teaches people to wait for the best view (higher means brighter and easier) and gives a reason to follow Show me early rather than grab it low on the horizon.

05Night ambience

A very quiet bed under the music: a little wind, crickets at dusk, an owl late, the first birds at dawn, rain when it's raining. It follows the real forecast and the time, so the phone sounds like the night you're standing in. I'd ship it on, at about −48 LUFS (well under the sound effects and the music), ducking with the music during a reveal.

The rule set

  • 1 · Answers always show. Anything that answers your tap (reminder set, couldn't save, purchase) shows at once.
  • 2 · The reveal carries its own news. Missions, rank, patches and progress earned by a catch are chips on that reveal, never a banner later.
  • 3 · Fold into what you'll see anyway. Patches and the meteor shower from the first catch go onto the Tonight page, which opens right after it.
  • 4 · Progress only at milestones. Constellations speak at halfway and complete; the first star of one is a line on its card (“1 of 5 in Lyra”), not a notice.
  • 5 · Help waits for evidence. The compass nudge needs two misses (a lock that slides off, a Not quite) and never comes before the first catch.
  • 6 · One unasked notice per five minutes. Extras queue; anything older than 15 min when its turn comes is dropped.
  • 7 · First of its kind gets a moment. Fits with Quick Commons: the first star, stage and Moon still reveal in full.

What I’d build first

If I could only do three, in this order.

  1. 06 · The notice rulesNo new art, mostly deleting and moving calls. It makes the first night calmer, and every other idea here adds a notice, so the rules should come first.
  2. 01 · Quick CommonsThe biggest change to how a night feels: more time aiming, less time tapping cards away. Commons are most of what people catch.
  3. 02 · What's worth aiming atSmall, and it makes the sky readable at a glance. It also makes 01 and 03 work better: you can see where the next new one is.
