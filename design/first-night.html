The first night

Seven ideas for the first few minutes: a hook before the tour starts, a tour that ends with a plan, and a daytime home that says what to do now. Each mock-up sits on a real capture of today’s app (Toronto, Oct 25), with the proposed part drawn on top.

How to read it: grey Today captions are the app as it is. Orange captions are the proposal. Numbers and times are examples. Effort: small is an afternoon, medium a day or two, large more. Animations are light and stop under Reduce Motion.

Hook in the first minute · before the tour, and the moment it ends

01A live number on the welcome card

The card says the sky is yours to collect, but it doesn’t prove there’s anything up there. One live count does: “312 satellites are above you right now.” It works by day too, because it counts everything above your horizon, sunlit or not, so it’s never zero. It ticks up and down a little as things rise and set. Before location is allowed it uses the time-zone city and says so.

02Day one ends with an appointment

Most first catches happen by day, on the Sun or the Moon. Today the close page then shows a whole timeline. Proposed: it leads with one concrete plan: “The Space Station passes over you tonight at 7:52 PM. We’ll tell you.” The big button sets the reminder (ISS reminders are always free), and the bell swings when it’s on. The rest of the timeline sits smaller underneath. No ISS tonight? 03If something big is close, start with it

When the ISS or a Rare-or-better pass rises within 15 minutes, the tour’s first assignment is that, not the Sun or Moon. The gold card counts down with the object in the circle. Waiting six minutes with nothing to do is the risk, so it offers a fallback: catch the Moon now, and a small pill keeps counting at the top. A minute before it rises, the arrow switches back to it.

The tour · one lesson, one less wall, and how we’d know

04Teach one thing after the first catch

Right after the first card lands, one small panel rises under it and teaches the reason to come back: “Come back on another night and this card levels up.” Bronze, silver, gold light up in turn, with your card ringed where it is now. One “Got it” and it’s gone. The catch: the Sun and Moon don’t level (since Oct 21), so for the Moon the lesson is its phases, and for the Sun it waits for your first satellite.

“Use my location / Not now” reads like a permission wall, and “Not now” sounds like you’ll miss something. Proposed: the second button is a real choice, a place picked from your phone’s time zone (we already do this). You can start right away either way, and change it in Settings later.

Clarity by day · what to do when it isn’t dark yet

If I had to order them

Small ones first, then the one with real logic in it.

  1. 01 · A live number (B)Cheapest way to make “every light is a card” feel true before anyone goes outside.
  2. 02 · An appointmentTurns a daytime first catch into a reason to open the app tonight. Measurable with one new row.
  3. 07 · A daytime home (B)Most first opens happen by day; this gives them something to do.
  4. 05 · Location optionalA copy change. Watch the tour finish rate.
  5. 04, then 0304 is small once the Sun/Moon cases are decided. 03 needs the tour to wait and switch targets, so it’s the riskiest.

Open questions: 01 needs a fast count of everything above the horizon (the catalogue sweep covers it, but before location it’s the time-zone city). 02 assumes an ISS pass tonight; late October in Toronto has none in the evening, so it would name the headliner instead. 04: natural cards have no levels, so a Sun first catch has nothing to level; I’d hold the lesson for the first satellite. 07: Venus only shows by day 30° or more from the Sun, and the Moon by day needs 15% lit; the chips list only what really qualifies. 03 and the B options cover more of the sky than today.
