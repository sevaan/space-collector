Explore, five ways

Five designers, one brief: make the moment of lining something up feel better, and get everything else out of the sky's way. Every phone uses the real sky from the app at the same moment (an Uncommon SL-8 stage in the circle). The circle itself doesn't change in any of them; what changes is everything around it.

Maya · AR and avionics HUDs

A · Glass cockpit

"A fighter HUD never puts a box over what you're looking at. Put the words on the target, not under it."

The radar becomes a heading tape across the top (where you face, with coloured ticks for new things around you). The name and distance hug the circle in corner brackets. Collect is a short bar right under it. The slider stands up on the right edge. No wordmark.

+ The most sky; feels like an instrument; the tape is easier to read than a tiny radar.

− Loses the "Space Collector" branding on the main screen; brackets get crowded on long names.

B · The card rises

"The card is the reward. Show it the second you lock on, before they've earned it, and they'll want it."

Locking on slides up a sheet with the real card picture tilted and glowing in its rarity, the name, what it is, the XP and a big Collect at thumb height. The header becomes your rank, XP bar and tonight's missions as four diamonds. The radar stays, smaller.

+ Most rewarding; Collect is where a thumb is; progress is always in view.

− The sheet covers the lower third of the sky while locked; shows the art before you catch it (today it's hidden until then).

C · Just the sky

Jonah · live games and quests

D · Tonight's run

"People open it outside with a goal: what's next, what's left. Give them a run to finish."

The top card is tonight's run: how many new cards you've caught of tonight's, the next pass with a countdown, and the three missions. New things off-screen get arrows on the screen's edge in their rarity colour with how far to turn (replacing the radar). Collect is a big round button for the right thumb, with a ring that fills as you hold steady.

+ Strongest "one more" pull; edge arrows teach turning without reading a radar.

− Busiest of the five; the run card needs good data every night (cloudy nights, daytime).

E · Horizon strip

"Every stargazer thinks in horizons. Show the whole horizon, not a little radar."

A slim version of All around you lives above the tabs: the horizon, your view as a dashed box, what's up in each direction with its rarity, turning live as you turn. The radar and Tonight chip go. Name, distance and what it is sit above the circle; Collect below. Wordmark kept, slimmer.

+ Best for finding things; reuses a screen you already built; keeps the brand.

− The strip and the circle compete for attention; less sky at the bottom.

The room's pick: C as the base, with D's edge arrows and B's card on lock.
  • From C: no wordmark or boxes over the sky; one small "5 up · 2 new" chip (opens All around you), Settings, and the tabs as a floating dock.
  • From D: rarity-coloured arrows on the screen's edge for new things you're not facing ("◆ Rare · 40° →"), instead of the radar.
  • From B: when something locks on, a short sheet rises with its card (picture still hidden until you catch it), name, XP and Collect at thumb height.
  • The sky slider stays on screen in every version (Sevaan uses it a lot); red night mode moves into Settings, out of the header.
Split votes: Theo wanted the rank and missions always visible (B's header); Maya argued for the heading tape (A) over edge arrows. Happy to mock the combined version next.
