Tonight, with fresh eyes

A room that had never seen the app: a live-service collecting-game product designer, a visual/interaction designer (Weather, Flighty, Strava school), and two players: Cas, who opens it a few nights a month, and Kee, who wants every card. We looked at the captures for Peterborough, Ontario on 29 Oct 2026: afternoon, dusk, Earth’s shadow and dawn.

Verdict. The page is accurate and well drawn, but it doesn’t have an opinion. Four different pickers answer “what’s best tonight”, and they disagree. Every pass shows up three or four times in different shapes. Below the fold is a list of 20 near-identical Uncommon rows. Cas can’t tell in five seconds whether to go out, when, or for what. Kee sees no chase, no progress and no reason why tonight is better than tomorrow. The parts of the night (dusk, Earth’s shadow, dawn) are a good idea and the countdown card is the best thing on the page. Build the first screen around one window, one chase and one next action, and push everything else down.

1 · What’s weak

  1. HierarchyFour pickers, four answers. At dusk (8:02 PM) the headline says Best bet: Space Station at 6:07 a.m., the hero card says Tonight’s headliner is the Uncommon SL-8 stage at 8:16 PM, and a Rare Radiation Sat (5E-1) passes at 8:05, three minutes away, as row 2 of the run. In the afternoon the ★ Headliner is Tiangong, but the lit row is Kosmos 2142 (Uncommon).
  2. RedundancyThe same pass, four times. Afternoon Tiangong shows in the headline, the chart gem, the Dusk plan row (Tiangong Space Station + 46 more) and its run row. At dawn the ISS is the headline, the hero, a 6:07 The headliner pin and a Dawn plan row. Dark time appears three times: Dark in 7 h 39 min · 6:42 p.m., the run’s 6:42 PM Dark enough to start and the button.
  3. First screenIn the afternoon there are no cards. It’s a collecting game, but the first screen at 11 AM is a sentence, a bar chart, three text rows and a button. The thing that sells the night (card art for what you could own by 8 PM) only appears at dusk.
  4. DensityThe run is a wall. 20+ rows about 83 pt tall, almost all ▲ Uncommon · high in the east · New card +15, each with two 44 pt round buttons (40+ identical circles). Names like OPS 6182 (DMSP 5D-1 F3) mean nothing to Cas, and nothing ranks them. Rare and Uncommon look the same apart from a small gem.
  5. Wrong scaleA plan written as a countdown. At 11 AM every row says in 8h 15m, in 8h 41m, in 18h 22m. Hours away, people need clock times and a window, not 20 countdowns.
  6. ShadowEarth’s shadow lectures and doesn’t act. At 1:03 AM the card spends six lines explaining shadow. The Moon row says Up in the east from about 1:00 a.m., so it’s up now, while the mission below says Collect a planet or the Moon · Look for one. There’s no Collect or Aim on the one thing you can actually catch.
  7. Contradiction“Dark until about 4:30” isn’t true. The same screen’s run starts with a lit SL-8 at 4:17 AM, and the missions say Next: 4:00 a.m. Kosmos 1589. The edge of the shadow and the first lit pass come from different sources.
  8. MissionsProgress sits three screens down and points elsewhere. Missions · 0 of 3 is the only progression on the page, and it’s at the very bottom. At dusk it suggests GSLV stage · 2025 and SJ-12, which aren’t in the hero or the run. The mission and the pass never meet on one row.
  9. MissingNothing about me, nothing to chase. There’s no collection count, no streak, no “1 night from Silver”, no caught-tonight tally and no rare drop. Live-service games sell tonight as scarce (“3 Rares you’ve never had; the ISS only before dawn”). Here every night looks the same.
  10. CopySmall things that read as unfinished: 7:20 p.m.. (double full stop); p.m. in the plan but PM in the run; Space Station + 41 more (which station?); · 1965 orphaned in the fade rush; two Over for tonight rows taking 240 pt at dawn; Busiest this evening listed at 7:43 and again at 8:04; Reminder free; the legend row spends a whole line on four ground lines.

Keep

The countdown cardThe real card art beside a big countdown and Show me (dusk and dawn captures) is the strongest moment on the page. Make it the default, not the exception.
Dusk / Earth’s shadow / DawnA true and teachable model of the night that no other sky app gives you. Keep it as the frame, but make it a thin strip rather than a chapter.
Missions that name a passNext: 6:44 p.m. Ariane 5 stage, east is exactly right. Move it onto the pass.
The fade rushA real skill moment with a shiny reward. As one chip with a count it’s exciting; as a two-column list it isn’t.
Free ISS reminderA generous hook. Say it as a benefit (“Free alert”), not a label.
The lookNavy, cream, orange, Russo + Barlow and the card frames are distinctive. The problems are structure and copy, not style.

2 · Make it magic

The owner is right: the sky view, the reveal, the welcome card and the collection all feel like a game, and this page feels like a report. A bar chart, a legend and 20 rows are the language of a weather app. The fix isn’t decoration. The page should show the night itself and the cards you could own by morning, and the “when to go out” answer should come out of that picture.

The sky is the heroThe top third of the page is a window onto tonight’s real sky: the gradient for the Sun’s height (Explore already saves skyTone), the app’s own pines and hills (stripLandscape), twinkling stars, the Milky Way photo at night and the forecast’s real drifting clouds. It changes through the day, so opening the page at 11 AM, 8 PM and 1 AM feels different.
Passes are paths, not rowsTonight’s best catches are drawn as glowing arcs across that sky, from where they rise to where they fade, each with its card riding on it. You can see the night’s shape at a glance: “two big ones in the southeast just after dark”.
Cards stay sealedThe chase is a hand of face-down cards in the Collection’s back, glowing in their rarity colour, each with a time. Anticipation is the most collectible feeling there is: you go out to turn them over. Caught ones flip face up on the page.
Times live in the pictureThe window is a glowing bracket on a dusk-to-dawn bar that looks like the sky (twilight blue, shadow black). No bars to count, no legend. The full chart moves to “All passes” for keen players.
Moon and planet lines: cut them from the chart, and cut the legend.

At 2 pt thick, pale and stacked at the foot of the chart, they read as a broken axis. They need a whole legend row to decode, and they answer a question nobody asks of a pass chart (“which hours is Saturn above 8°?”). The Moon and planets matter at two moments: by day, as “what you can catch tonight even if satellites go quiet”, and in Earth’s shadow, as the stars of the show. So draw them as themselves: a glowing Moon disc and coloured planet dots in the sky scene, each with a rise time (“Jupiter 2:30”). In the shadow part they become the hero card. The one exception is a bright Moon washing out the sky, which deserves one line of copy (“Bright Moon after 10 PM: fainter ones vanish”), not a line on a chart.

3 · A better first screen

One rule for every moment: the sky as it is (or will be) → the cards in it → one next action. One picker (rarest new card, then soonest) feeds the headline, the hero, the sky arcs and the notification. The full run moves behind “All passes”. The dashed red line is the fold on a 390 × 844 phone. Counts the captures don’t show (window size, cards in the window) are illustrative. On a phone the stars twinkle and clouds drift.
