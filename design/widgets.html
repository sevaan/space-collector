The app writes tonight's plan (next passes, dark and dawn, the forecast) when it plans the night; the widgets count down on their own from it, with no app open. How the small one changes through a day

Same widget, six moments. Countdowns tick live (WidgetKit's built-in timer text and progress); the rest changes on the schedule the app hands it.

Lock screen

Lock-screen widgets are drawn by iOS in one tint over your wallpaper, so they're shapes and words, no colour or art. Three kinds: a circle, a rectangle, and the line above the clock.

Notes

  • No app open needed for the countdowns. The app saves tonight's next ~12 passes, dark, dawn and the forecast for the widget whenever it plans the night (already happens on launch and every 20 min while open). The widget steps through them by itself, so it stays right all night.
  • It can go stale. If the app hasn't been opened for a day or two there's no plan for tonight; the widget then says "Open Space Collector to plan tonight" (state 6). iOS also refreshes the forecast only when the app runs.
  • Tap targets. Small and lock screen: one tap opens Show me for that pass. Medium and large: each row opens its own pass. Buttons inside a widget (say, a bell that sets the reminder) are possible on iOS 17+, but I.d leave them out at first.
  • Same pick as the app. "Next" uses the app's own rule (the headliner while it's ahead, then the best new catch), so the widget and the Tonight page never disagree. Commons only once you're nearly out of Uncommons.
  • Apple Watch for nearly free. The lock-screen circle and rectangle are the same widget kinds as Watch complications; once these exist they can be put on a watch face with little extra work.
  • Plus (decided 2026-10-22). Every widget, home screen and lock screen, comes with Plus. Without it a widget shows what Plus is and opens the Plus sheet; the widget gallery shows a sample night.
  • Build cost. A native WidgetKit extension in the iOS project (Swift, not web), plus a small bridge so the web app can hand it the plan. Android would be a separate Glance widget later.
